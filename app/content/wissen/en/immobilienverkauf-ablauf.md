A sale is not a single moment but a sequence of decisions. Knowing the order helps you negotiate more calmly. This page describes the usual process in Switzerland, without promising timelines that depend on the property and the market.

## 1. Preparation

Before a property becomes visible, the documents should be complete: land register extract, plans, building description, building insurance certificate, and for condominium property the regulations, the latest minutes and the statements. Interested parties ask for exactly these documents, and a tidy file saves weeks later on.

Also clarify who is selling. For communities of heirs or co-ownership, all parties involved must agree before an offer becomes binding.

## 2. Pricing

An asking price arises from comparison, condition and location. It is an assessment, not a guarantee. Set too high, a property loses attention in the first weeks; set too low, it gives away room for negotiation. Anyone who wants a second opinion can [request a no-obligation valuation from Fourwalls](/en/bewertung). It takes place in conversation and does not state an automatically calculated figure.

## 3. Marketing

Text, photos and floor plans are what interested parties see first. A listing should state the facts in full: floor area, rooms, year of construction, condition, service charges, availability. Whether you [advertise it yourself or hand marketing over to Fourwalls](/en/wissen/selbst-inserieren-oder-mit-fourwalls) is a question of time and experience, not of the property's quality.

## 4. Viewings and negotiation

Viewings are conversations. Interested parties ask about renovations, noise, the neighbourhood, heating and the reason for selling. Honest answers prevent later disputes. Offers often come with conditions, such as a bank's financing commitment. Check these conditions before agreeing.

## 5. Notarisation and the land register

In Switzerland, a contract of sale for land is only valid if it is notarised. The notary's office prepares the contract, both parties sign, and ownership passes upon entry in the land register. Between signing and entry there is usually payment, the repayment of existing mortgages and settlement of the property gains tax, whose rate varies by canton.
