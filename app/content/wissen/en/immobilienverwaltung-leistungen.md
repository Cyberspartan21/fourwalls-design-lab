"Management" is an umbrella term. Behind it are individual services that carry different weight depending on the property. This page explains them, so you know what to ask for.

## Day-to-day administration

The ongoing care of the property: rent collection, reminders, communication with authorities and insurers, keeping the property's records. Day-to-day administration is the core of a management mandate.

## Letting

Re-letting after terminations: listing, viewings, reviewing applications, signing the contract, handover. For new buildings this is called first letting.

## Tenant support

A point of contact for tenants: damage reports, questions about the contract, neighbourhood matters, move-outs and handovers. Good support reduces turnover and vacancy.

## Statements

Heating and service charge statements for tenants, property statements for owners, receipts for the tax return. For condominium property, administration of the association is added, with the assembly, minutes and the renovation fund.

## Maintenance and coordination

Arranging minor repairs, obtaining quotes, coordinating tradespeople, planning and accompanying renovations. Management decides within the agreed authority and presents larger projects to the owners.

## Owner advice

Questions about rent adjustments, investments, vacancies or the property's long-term strategy. Advice complements day-to-day administration; it does not replace it.

## How a mandate comes about

At Fourwalls, a management mandate begins with a [request](/en/verwalten/anfrage), in which you describe the property, the number of units, current occupancy and the services you want. The scope is clarified in conversation. Based on the property and the desired scope of services, Fourwalls draws up an individual offer; the services provided are agreed to fit the property and the mandate. There is no standard package that automatically includes everything.

## How to recognise good management

- You can reach a contact person who knows your property.
- Statements are traceable and arrive on time.
- You hear about problems before they become expensive.
- Decisions about your money are made with you, not for you.
