Good flats attract many applications. Anyone who is prepared can submit an application on the day of the viewing. This page lists the usual documents and the most important rules, without representing individual landlords.

## The usual documents

- The management's application form, filled in completely.
- A current debt collection register extract, usually no older than three months, available from the debt collection office of your place of residence.
- A copy of your ID or residence permit.
- Details of your employer and income; some managements ask for a payslip or an employment confirmation.
- A reference from your previous landlord, if available.

Managements may only ask for information needed for the letting decision. You do not have to answer questions about religion, health or family planning.

## What makes an application convincing

Completeness and availability. An application with a missing extract is often not even reviewed. A short, factual covering letter helps when your situation needs explaining, for example a change of job or a move from abroad.

## Viewing

At the viewing, check what the listing does not show: noise at different times of day, the condition of the kitchen and bathroom, the heating system, cellar, laundry room, parking. Ask about the service charges and whether they are billed on account or as a flat rate.

## Tenancy agreement and deposit

The rental deposit for residential premises may not exceed three months' rent and must be paid into a blocked account in your name. It belongs to you and is released after moving out, provided there are no justified claims.

The notice period for flats is at least three months, ending on a locally customary date; the contract and the canton determine the details. Read the whole contract before signing, including the general terms and the handover record.
